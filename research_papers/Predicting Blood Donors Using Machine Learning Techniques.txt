The United States’ blood supply chain is experiencing market decline due to recent innovations in surgical
 practice, transfusion management, and hospital policy. These innovations strain US blood centers, resulting
 in cuts to surge capacities, consolidation, and reduced funding for research and outreach programs. In this 
study, we use data from a regional blood center to explore the application of contemporary machine learning
 algorithms for modeling donor retention. Such predictive models of donor retention can be used to design more
 cost effective donor outreach programs. Using data from a large US blood center paired with random forest 
classifiers, we are able to build a model of donor retention with a Mathews correlation of coefficient of 0.851.
