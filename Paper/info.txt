Title: Decentralized and Innovative Resource Mobilization Strategies for Healthcare Resilience During Crises: A Systematic Literature Review.



RQ1- What decentralized and innovative blood resource mobilization strategies have been implemented to support 
healthcare systems during crises?

RQ2- How can regional blood-group distribution data be integrated into decentralized blood resource mobilization
strategies?

RQ3- How can regional blood-group data support the identification and mobilization of suitable blood donors 
during emergencies?

RQ4- What role can data-driven approaches play in matching regional blood supply with emergency blood demand 
across different blood groups?

RQ5- What operational, logistical, ethical, and data-related challenges affect the implementation of these 
strategies?


--------------------
(Selection Criteria):

-Examine blood donation, blood collection, blood inventory, blood supply, blood distribution, transfusion services, 
or blood-resource mobilization.

-Publications were required to address crises, disasters,resilience, robustness or disruptions 
affecting blood donation services.

-Only peer-reviewed articles published in English between 2020 and 2026 were included. 

-Investigate distributed or community-based approaches, such as Walking Blood Banks, emergency donor networks, mobile blood collection,
regional donor registries, or cooperation between blood banks.

-Examine new or adapted operational, organizational, technological, or logistical approaches to mobilizing blood resources.

-Investigate statistical models, optimization, forecasting, geographic information systems (GIS), artificial intelligence, machine learning,
or decision-support systems for blood supply and demand.


-include a paper if

1. It satisfies the mandatory publication and relevance requirements.
2. It provides meaningful evidence for at least one research question.
3. It contains enough information for data extraction.


SLR Assumption: 
Studies were selected based on predefined inclusion and exclusion criteria. Not all inclusion criteria were 
required to be simultaneously satisfied, as the review addresses multiple interconnected research questions.
Studies were considered eligible if they met the mandatory publication and relevance requirements and contributed
meaningful evidence to at least one research question. Depending on their focus, studies could address 
decentralized blood-resource mobilization, regional blood-group distribution, donor identification and 
mobilization, data-driven blood supply and demand matching, or implementation challenges. Foundational 
studies addressing blood-group distributions and donor characteristics were also considered, even when 
they did not explicitly examine crisis situations, provided that their findings were relevant to the 
development of emergency blood-resource mobilization strategies.
